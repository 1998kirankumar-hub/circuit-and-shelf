# The true cost of owning a console in India goes beyond the MRP

When a gaming console goes on sale, the discount on the box gets most of the attention. But the actual cost of gaming is the sum of the machine, the games you play, the accessories you buy, any memberships you renew and the value you recover if you eventually sell it.

This guide gives you a straightforward ownership worksheet. It deliberately avoids pretending that one current store price will remain accurate for every reader.

## 1. Hardware is the starting point

Record the delivered price for the exact console model you want. Include the version, bundled controller count, disc-drive availability and storage capacity. Two advertisements using the same console family name may refer to meaningfully different packages.

Consider whether your television already supports the display modes you care about. You don't need to replace a perfectly good TV merely to enjoy console gaming, but upgrading one can dwarf the cost of the console itself.

## 2. Games are often the biggest variable

List the five to ten games you're most likely to buy during the first year. Price each one realistically—not at its all-time lowest historical sale price unless you are willing to wait. If you buy discs, consider delivery charges and whether the local used-game market offers reliable resale or trade options.

If you play only one or two major games a year, owning those games may be economical. If you experiment widely, a subscription catalog might suit you better. Both strategies can be smart; the mistake is paying for subscriptions and buying multiple full-price titles you never finish.

## 3. Accessories quietly add up

An extra controller is useful for multiplayer, but it isn't essential for everyone. The same applies to charging docks, headsets, SSD expansion, stands, decorative covers and premium controllers.

Make a **needed-now** list and a **nice-later** list. Buy the latter only when you have repeatedly encountered a limitation. In particular, understand storage compatibility and installation requirements before ordering an expansion drive.

## 4. Subscriptions are recurring commitments

A monthly fee looks small in isolation. Multiply it by twelve and compare the annual cost with the games and services you actually use. Check cancellation rules, catalog changes, membership tiers and what happens to game access when your subscription lapses.

For online multiplayer, confirm which titles and modes require an active subscription on your chosen platform. Don't assume the same rules apply to every free-to-play or paid game.

## 5. Leave room for ownership risk

Electronics can need repairs outside warranty. Keep the original invoice, maintain ventilation, protect the console from unstable power where appropriate and understand local service options. Don't assume a manufacturer's global warranty applies identically to every imported or grey-market unit.

If buying used, allocate a repair-risk buffer. A low purchase price can quickly become less appealing when a controller and HDMI port both need attention.

## 6. Resale is a bonus, not a guarantee

Physical discs may offer flexibility to trade or resell where lawful and practical. Consoles can also have second-hand value, but condition, warranty, availability and future launches affect demand. Treat expected resale as a conservative range, not money you already have.

For digital software, never assume you can sell the game independently of the account. Digital purchase and license terms are platform-specific.

## 7. Use this simple first-year formula

**First-year ownership cost = hardware + games + subscription + accessories + repair buffer − realistic resale proceeds.**

Try three scenarios in a notes app:

- **Budget:** one controller, selected discounted games, no unnecessary accessories.
- **Balanced:** a handful of new releases and a membership you use consistently.
- **Enthusiast:** more games, an extra controller, premium accessories and expanded storage.

Build the estimate for both candidate consoles with the same assumptions. If one option costs less but does not offer the games you want, that saving may be irrelevant. If both offer the same experiences for you, total cost becomes a powerful tiebreaker.

## Our verdict

The right console isn't always the cheapest unit on launch day or during a sale. It's the one that delivers the experiences you care about at a **total ownership cost you can comfortably accept**.
